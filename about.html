Some stuff about me

My Software Engineering Principles

A Brief Summary of My Background I was born in Malaysia in 2003 but my family quickly moved to Thailand for work when I was just 3 months old, and there I spent my childhood. My family moved again when I was 9, to Saudi Arabia, where I spent my middle school and high school years. My parents are both academics who have studied and worked abroad for the majority of their lives. I believe that this imparted quite an impression on me growing up, giving me a much better understanding of the rest of the world and its diversity compared to most of my peers.

In the summer of 2019 when I had just finished my second year of high school and my IGCSEs, I was at a crossroads. I could have just stayed on and continued to pursue A-levels for 2 years before starting university but a brief stroke of luck and circumstance led to me finding out about some foundation years that would let me in with just IGCSEs, instead of requiring me to have finished high school. This would mean that I could start university a year early, so I started applying for foundation years during the summer while I was traveling, mere weeks before the start dates of the programs I was applying to.

By this time, I was 16 and had been to well over half a dozen countries in my life, but never spent much more than a few weeks on holiday in my home country of Malaysia. Therefore, when I started getting offers from universities in Malaysia, I was thrilled at the opportunity to actually spend some time in my home country after a lifetime spent abroad. I chose the University of Southampton as it is ranked highly for computer science and has historically always had a strong focus and investment in electronics and computer science. By September 2019, I had moved out and started my Engineering Foundation Year at 16 at the University of Southampton's Malaysia Campus. A year later, I was headed off to the UK as the only one in my cohort of around 100 people to pursue computer science instead of an engineering degree.

How I got into computing Like many before me, I first dabbled into tech from gaming. I was extremely frustrated at trying to get minecraft to run on my dual-core toaster of a laptop and fell down the rabbit hole of learning about how computer games and programs in general ran on the underlying hardware. Eventually, this led to the enthusiast PC community which taught me even more about consumer PC hardware and helped me pick out the parts for and assemble my first computer when I was 12.

When I was 14 and in my first year of high school, I was talking to a friend of mine who mentioned he was learning how to write a prime factorization algorithm in Python. I remember thinking to myself that, while I was quite tech literate on the hardware side of things, it was quite embarrassing that I hadn't even written a single line of code. As a kid whose closest experience to programming was writing some basic HTML and CSS for a wordpress-like blog, I didn't even know where to start. I remember following this https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rfscVS0vtbw specific 4 hour python tutorial when I got home from school and the feeling of pride and satisfaction when my little program finally worked.

A side note, I also remember the disappointment I felt when I realized how slow and inefficient my program was, and I spent the rest of the night trying (unsuccessfully) to try and optimize my program. I knew nothing about algorithmics or optimization of course, and if anything, just made the program worse. Looking back at this story now, it's obvious to anyone who's done an algorithmics class or learned about computational complexity why my younger self had failed so spectacularly in optimizing this “simple” problem
